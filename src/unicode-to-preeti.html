Step-by-Step Guide: Converting Unicode to Preeti Font

Although modern computing platforms, search engines, and websites require **Nepali Unicode**, many traditional design applications (such as PageMaker, InDesign, and CorelDRAW) used by printing presses and publishing houses in Nepal still rely on legacy ASCII-based fonts like **Preeti**, **Kantipur**, and **Sagarmatha**. This requires converting Unicode Devanagari back to legacy Preeti layouts.

Why is the Reverse Conversion Necessary?

Legacy publishing programs often have issues rendering standard Devanagari Unicode correctly (failing with broken conjuncts or displaced matras). In such cases, designers convert Unicode text back into Preeti representation, install the legacy Preeti font on their OS, and style the text block to render it perfectly in print formats.

The Unicode-to-Preeti Reordering Rules

Since Unicode stores characters in logical phonetic order while legacy fonts rely on visual typing order, our converter performs complex transformations:

  • Reph Reordering (र्): In Unicode, the half-R sound is typed first (e.g. र् + म in धर्म). The converter identifies this sequence, shifts the half-R indicator to the end of the consonant cluster, and maps it to the Preeti { glyph.
  • Short 'i' Matra (ि) Placement: In Devanagari Unicode, the ि diacritic is logically written after the consonant it modifies. Preeti visual layout expects it to be typed before the consonant. The engine identifies every consonant cluster followed by a ि, moves the ि to the front, and translates it to the Preeti l character.
  • Compound Glyph Translation: Large conjunct forms (like द्घ, क्ष्, श्र) are replaced with their respective custom keyboard mapping characters (such as ¡, I, >) by running through our sorted dictionary.
